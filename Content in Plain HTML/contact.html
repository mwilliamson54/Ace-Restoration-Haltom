When to contact a restoration professional

Contact us when water is actively spreading through a property, a plumbing failure has soaked flooring or walls, rain has entered through a roof or opening, a fire has left smoke or soot residue, or suspected mold requires professional attention. You may also want to speak with a restoration team after the immediate emergency has passed if materials remain damp, an odor persists, or you are unsure whether damage extends behind a visible surface.

Some situations require another professional first. A plumber should address a failed water line or fixture. An electrician should assess electrical hazards. A roofer may be needed to repair the roof opening that allowed rain inside. Fire and emergency personnel must make a fire scene safe before restoration work begins. Restoration can then address the affected building materials and coordinate the appropriate next steps within the agreed scope.

What information should you have ready?

You do not need a complete damage report before making an urgent call. A few practical details can help us understand the situation:

  • Property location: the street address, city, and ZIP code. This helps confirm the service area and practical dispatch arrangements.
  • Type of problem: water damage, burst pipe, appliance leak, storm intrusion, fire damage, smoke or soot, suspected mold, or another issue.
  • Whether the source is active: for example, whether water is still flowing or the roof is still allowing rain inside.
  • Areas affected: rooms, floors, ceilings, walls, crawlspaces, attics, storage areas, or commercial workspaces.
  • When it began: the approximate time the issue started or when you first noticed it.
  • Safety concerns: standing water near electricity, sewage, structural instability, smoke, chemical odors, or restricted access.
  • Property use: owner-occupied home, rental, apartment, office, retail space, warehouse, or another building type.

If you can safely take photographs from a dry and secure location, they may help explain visible conditions. Do not enter a dangerous area or delay an emergency call to collect pictures or paperwork.

Emergency water damage: what to do before the restoration visit

If a pipe or appliance is leaking, turn off the fixture or main water supply only if the shutoff is safely accessible. Do not enter standing water where electrical outlets, appliances, wiring, or a breaker panel may be affected. Keep children, pets, tenants, and employees away from unsafe areas. If water may be contaminated with sewage or floodwater, avoid contact and wait for qualified professionals to assess the situation.

Once the source is controlled and the area is safe, make a note of which rooms and materials are affected. Avoid pulling up flooring, cutting drywall, or removing insulation before the condition has been assessed. These materials may need a specific handling or documentation process. For a detailed explanation of extraction, moisture assessment, structural drying, and dehumidification, read our water damage restoration guide.

After a fire: safety comes before cleanup

Do not re-enter a fire-damaged building until the fire department or appropriate authority says it is safe. Even after flames are out, a property may have structural damage, unstable materials, electrical hazards, soot, smoke residue, or water from firefighting. Avoid wiping soot from surfaces or switching on ventilation equipment without understanding the condition; improper cleaning can spread residue or complicate restoration.

When the scene has been released for appropriate work, a restoration assessment can consider the areas affected by flames, smoke migration, soot deposits, odor, and firefighting water. Visit our fire and smoke damage restoration page to learn more about the service scope.

Suspected mold: avoid disturbing the affected area

If you notice visible growth, recurring dampness, or a musty odor, do not scrape, sand, or aggressively clean a large or uncertain affected area. Disturbing mold-contaminated materials can spread particles and may expose occupants. The source of moisture should be identified and addressed, and the appropriate professional assessment or remediation must follow Texas requirements.

Texas has separate regulatory requirements for mold assessment and mold remediation. Depending on the project, the assessment and remediation roles may need to be performed by different licensed parties. Ace Restoration's mold-related work is subject to the applicable licensing framework. Read our mold removal and remediation information for a broader explanation.

We keep restoration separate from specialist trade work

Plumbing, electrical, roofing, engineering, and permitted construction work may require separate licensed professionals. A restoration project can involve several disciplines, and the responsible party for each part should be understood before work begins.

Questions about estimates, insurance, and documentation

Customers often want to know how much a project will cost, whether insurance will pay, and how long the work will take. Those questions are reasonable, but a reliable answer depends on the actual damage. The amount of water, category of water, affected materials, building layout, access, drying requirements, contents, and reconstruction scope can all affect cost and duration.

Insurance coverage depends on the policy language, cause of loss, exclusions, limits, deductibles, and the insurer's decision. A restoration company should not promise that a claim will be approved. Contact your insurer to understand reporting requirements and keep copies of relevant correspondence, photographs, and project documents. Ask the restoration team what documentation it can provide for the work it performs.
