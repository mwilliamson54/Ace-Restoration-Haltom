Common water-loss causes

  • Leaking plumbing lines and fixture connections.
  • Washing machine, dishwasher, refrigerator, and water heater failures.
  • Toilet, sink, and tub overflows affecting floors and connected rooms.
  • Roof leaks around flashing, vents, and other penetrations.
  • Rainwater entering through doors, windows, low openings, or damaged roofing.
  • Sewer backups or wastewater problems requiring contamination-aware cleanup.

The EPA recommends drying wet or damp materials within 24 to 48 hours when possible to reduce the chance of mold growth. This is a prevention target, not a guarantee. Concealed materials may need to be assessed and the original moisture source must be corrected.

Mold removal and remediation in Sansom Park

Mold may develop when moisture remains in drywall, insulation, wood, flooring, or other building materials after a leak, roof intrusion, condensation, or water event. Growth may be visible or hidden behind walls, beneath flooring, inside cabinets, or in attic and crawl-space materials. A musty odor or recurring discoloration can justify investigating moisture, but color alone cannot identify a mold species.

Effective remediation addresses both the moisture source and affected materials. Depending on the project, work may include containment, controlled removal, cleaning, drying, and repairs by the appropriate trade. Texas regulates mold assessment and remediation through TDLR. The same licensee generally cannot perform both regulated assessment and remediation activities on the same project, subject to a narrow school-district exception. Verify current credentials and confirm the separate responsibilities of the professionals involved.

What to do after water or fire damage

  1. Protect people first. Leave if there is smoke, suspected gas, structural damage, or another immediate danger.
  2. Avoid unsafe water. Do not enter standing water where electrical equipment or wiring may be affected.
  3. Stop the source only if safe. Use an accessible water shutoff only when it can be reached without risk; otherwise contact a qualified plumber or property manager.
  4. Document visible damage. Take photographs from a safe location and note the time and likely source.
  5. Notify the responsible parties. Contact your insurer, landlord, property manager, or facilities contact as appropriate.
  6. Describe possible contamination. Tell the restoration provider if the water may involve sewage, outdoor runoff, or another contaminated source.
